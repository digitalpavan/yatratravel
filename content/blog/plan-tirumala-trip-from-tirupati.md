For most pilgrims, a visit to Tirumala begins in Tirupati, at the foot of the Seshachalam hills. Getting up to the temple of Sri Venkateswara Swamy is simple once you know the options, and a little planning makes the day far calmer, especially with elders or children.

## Getting up the hill

There are two main ways to reach Tirumala from Tirupati: by road through the ghat, or on foot along one of the traditional footpaths.

### By vehicle on the ghat road

Most visitors travel up by car, taxi or bus. Tirumala has separate ghat roads for going up and coming down, which keeps traffic moving in one direction on each. The climb from Alipiri is roughly 20 km of hairpin bends through forest, and usually takes somewhere around 45 minutes to an hour, depending on traffic and checks.

A few things are worth knowing:

- The roads are steep and winding. If anyone is prone to motion sickness, a light meal beforehand helps.
- Speed limits and rules on the ghat are strictly enforced.
- Access for certain vehicles, such as two-wheelers, may be restricted at some hours. Check the current rules before you set out.

### The Alipiri checkpoint

Every vehicle heading up passes through the security checkpoint at Alipiri. Vehicles and luggage are inspected, and passengers may be asked to step out while bags are scanned. On busy days there can be a queue of vehicles, so allow some extra time.

Items that are not permitted on the hill, such as alcohol, tobacco, non-vegetarian food and certain other goods, will be stopped here. It is easiest to leave anything doubtful behind in Tirupati.

### On foot: Alipiri and Srivari Mettu

Walking up is an act of devotion for many pilgrims, and there are two well-used footpaths:

- **Alipiri Mettu** starts at Alipiri, close to the town. It is the longer route, with several thousand steps, and can take a good part of a day depending on your pace and how often you rest.
- **Srivari Mettu** starts near Srinivasa Mangapuram, some distance outside Tirupati. It is shorter and steeper, and many people find it quicker to complete.

Both paths have shelters, drinking water points and shops along the way. Walk in comfortable footwear (or barefoot, if that is your vow), start early to avoid the midday heat, and carry a little water. Footpath timings and any related darshan arrangements can change, so check the latest information from TTD before you go.

## Booking darshan

Darshan at Tirumala is managed by Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD). Booking options, quotas, timings and rules change from time to time, so always rely on the official source:

- Visit TTD's official website at [ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in](https://ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in) for current darshan types, booking windows and accommodation.
- Book only through TTD's official channels. Be wary of anyone offering tickets or "special access" through unofficial routes.
- Keep a copy of your booking and the ID proof you used, as these are usually checked.

If you have not been able to book in advance, read the current guidance on the TTD website about free darshan and the queue system, and be prepared for waits that vary a great deal by day and season.

## What to carry and what to leave behind

### Worth carrying

- Your darshan booking confirmation and original photo ID
- Traditional clothing: TTD expects traditional dress for darshan, so check the current dress code before you travel
- A small bag with water, any medicines you need and a light shawl, as the hills can be cool in the early morning
- Some cash for small purchases, prasadam and offerings

### Best left behind

- Mobile phones, cameras and other electronic items are not allowed inside the main temple. There are deposit counters, but carrying less makes life easier.
- Prohibited items such as alcohol, tobacco and non-vegetarian food, which are not allowed on the hill at all
- Valuables and large amounts of jewellery
- Heavy luggage, if you are only going up for the day

Rules can change, so it is sensible to check TTD's current guidelines a few days before your visit.

## Timing your trip

Tirumala is busy throughout the year, but crowds are usually heavier on weekends, public holidays, school holidays and during major festivals such as Brahmotsavam and Vaikuntha Ekadasi. If your dates are flexible, a weekday outside the festival season is often more relaxed.

Some practical timing tips:

- Start from Tirupati early. Checkpoint queues and ghat traffic tend to build as the day goes on.
- Leave a margin before your darshan slot to cover the drive, security checks and getting to the queue.
- Plan a buffer on the way back, too, particularly if you have a train or flight to catch.
- If you are visiting other temples in Tirupati, it is common to see Tirumala first and the local temples afterwards.

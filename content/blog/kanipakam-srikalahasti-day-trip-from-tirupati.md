Many pilgrims who come to Tirupati also want to visit two other famous temples in the region: Sri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Swamy at Kanipakam and Sri Kalahasteeswara Swamy at Srikalahasti. Both can be covered in a single day by car from Tirupati, as long as you start early and plan the route sensibly.

Here is what to expect at each temple, how far they are and a route that works well.

## Kanipakam: Sri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Swamy Temple

### What it is known for

Kanipakam is home to a temple of Lord Ganesha, worshipped here as Sri Varasiddhi Vinayaka. The idol is believed to be self-manifested (swayambhu) and, according to local belief, has been slowly growing over time. The temple is built around a well, and water from it is considered sacred.

The temple is also known for a long tradition of devotees taking solemn oaths before the deity, which is said to settle disputes. Many families visit Kanipakam before starting something new, as Ganesha is worshipped as the remover of obstacles.

### How far it is

Kanipakam is about 70 km from Tirupati, near Chittoor. By car, the drive usually takes somewhere around one and a half to two hours, depending on traffic and stops.

## Srikalahasti: Sri Kalahasteeswara Swamy Temple

### What it is known for

Srikalahasti is one of the most important Shiva temples in South India. The lingam here is worshipped as the Vayu lingam, representing the element of air, and the temple is counted among the Pancha Bhoota Sthalams, the five temples linked with the five elements.

The temple sits on the banks of the Swarnamukhi river, with hills rising behind it. It is especially well known for Rahu-Ketu pooja, which many devotees perform here. The name of the town is linked to the legend of a spider (sri), a snake (kala) and an elephant (hasti), each of whom worshipped Lord Shiva at this spot.

### How far it is

Srikalahasti is about 36 km from Tirupati. The drive usually takes roughly an hour.

## A suggested route

Kanipakam and Srikalahasti lie in roughly opposite directions from Tirupati: Kanipakam to the south-west and Srikalahasti to the north-east. That means the day loops out and back on one side, through Tirupati, and then out and back on the other.

A route that works well for many visitors:

1. **Leave Tirupati early in the morning** and drive to Kanipakam.
2. **Darshan at Kanipakam**, then a break for breakfast or an early lunch.
3. **Drive back towards Tirupati** and continue on to Srikalahasti.
4. **Darshan at Srikalahasti**, and pooja if you have planned one.
5. **Return to Tirupati** in the evening.

Many devotees prefer to visit Srikalahasti last. There is a widely followed custom that after performing Rahu-Ketu pooja at Srikalahasti, one should go straight home rather than visit other temples. If this matters to you or your family, plan Srikalahasti as your final stop.

In all, the day involves a fair amount of driving, so expect to spend most of it on the road and at the temples. It is a full but very manageable day.

## Practical tips

- **Start early.** An early start gives you time for both temples without rushing and helps you avoid the heat of the afternoon.
- **Check timings and pooja details in advance.** Darshan hours, pooja arrangements and any charges change from time to time, and can be different on festival days. Check the current details with each temple's official sources before you set out, rather than relying on old information.
- **Dress traditionally.** Traditional, modest clothing is expected at both temples.
- **Phones and cameras.** Photography is generally not allowed inside the temples, and you may be asked to deposit phones. Follow the instructions at the entrance.
- **Carry water and snacks.** There are places to eat near both temples, but having water in the car is useful on a long day.
- **Expect crowds on special days.** Vinayaka Chaturthi brings very large crowds to Kanipakam, and Maha Shivaratri does the same at Srikalahasti. Mondays and eclipse days can also be busy at Srikalahasti.
- **Wear easy footwear.** You will be leaving shoes outside, so slip-on sandals make life simpler.
- **Keep the evening free.** After a long day, it is wise not to plan anything demanding for the same night.

## Combining with Tirumala

This day trip fits neatly into a Tirupati itinerary either before or after your Tirumala darshan. Trying to add Tirumala on the same day is not practical. If you have three days, many pilgrims do Tirumala on one day, the local Tirupati temples on another and Kanipakam and Srikalahasti on the third.

Many pilgrims come to Tirupati for darshan at Tirumala and leave without seeing the temples in the town below. That is a pity, because several of them are closely linked to the story of Lord Venkateswara and are easy to cover in a single day by car.

Here is a simple guide to five temples that fit comfortably into one day, with a suggested order and some practical tips.

## The temples

### Sri Padmavathi Ammavari Temple, Tiruchanur

Tiruchanur lies roughly 5 km from the centre of Tirupati. The temple is dedicated to Goddess Padmavathi, the consort of Lord Venkateswara. By tradition, many devotees visit her temple as part of their Tirumala pilgrimage, and some believe the pilgrimage is not complete without it.

The temple can be busy, particularly on Fridays and during festivals, so allow a little more time here.

### Sri Govindaraja Swamy Temple

In the heart of Tirupati, a short distance from the railway station, the Govindaraja Swamy Temple is one of the oldest and largest temples in the town. Its tall gopuram is a landmark you will notice as you move around the city. The main deity is Lord Govindaraja, shown in a reclining posture.

Because it is in the busy centre, parking and traffic can take some patience. An early or late visit is usually easier.

### Sri Kapileswara Swamy Temple

Kapileswara Swamy Temple is a Shiva temple at the foot of the Tirumala hills, on the northern edge of Tirupati. It is known for the Kapila Theertham, a sacred pool fed by a waterfall coming down from the hills. The waterfall is at its best after good rains and may be little more than a trickle in the dry months.

It is a peaceful stop and a nice contrast to the busier Vaishnava temples on the list.

### Sri Kalyana Venkateswara Swamy Temple, Srinivasa Mangapuram

Srinivasa Mangapuram is a short drive west of Tirupati, roughly 12 km away. According to tradition, Lord Venkateswara stayed here after his marriage to Goddess Padmavathi before going up to Tirumala. For this reason, the temple is especially popular with newly married couples and families seeking blessings for a marriage.

The Srivari Mettu footpath to Tirumala begins not far from here, so you may see pilgrims setting out on foot.

### ISKCON Tirupati

The ISKCON temple, dedicated to Sri Sri Radha Govinda, is on the road towards Alipiri. It is clean, spacious and calm, with a pleasant atmosphere for families. Many visitors like to end their day here, and the temple often has prasadam and a small shop.

## A suggested order

The exact order depends on where you are staying and what time you start, but this sequence works well for many visitors and keeps backtracking to a minimum:

1. **Sri Padmavathi Ammavari Temple, Tiruchanur**: start early, before the crowds build.
2. **Sri Govindaraja Swamy Temple**: head back into the town centre.
3. **Sri Kapileswara Swamy Temple**: move north to the foot of the hills.
4. **ISKCON Tirupati**: a short drive towards Alipiri, a good stop for a break.
5. **Sri Kalyana Venkateswara Swamy Temple, Srinivasa Mangapuram**: the drive out west makes a calm end to the day.

If you prefer, you can swap Srinivasa Mangapuram and ISKCON so that you finish closer to your hotel. Your driver can also suggest changes depending on traffic and temple timings on the day.

Allow most of a day for all five temples. Queues vary widely, so it is best not to try to fit too much else around this tour.

## Practical tips

- **Check timings before you go.** Each temple has its own darshan and closing hours, and these can change on festival days. Your best source is TTD's official website at [ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in](https://ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in), as several of these temples are administered by TTD.
- **Dress traditionally.** Modest, traditional clothing is appropriate at all these temples.
- **Phones and cameras.** Photography is generally not allowed inside the sanctum, and some temples may ask you to deposit phones. Follow the instructions at each entrance.
- **Footwear.** You will be taking your shoes off and putting them back on many times. Slip-on sandals save effort.
- **Carry water and small change.** Days in Tirupati can be hot, and small notes are handy for offerings and prasadam.
- **Eat at sensible times.** Plan a meal break around midday, perhaps between the town-centre temples and the drive out to Srinivasa Mangapuram.
- **Pace yourself with elders.** Some temples involve a fair amount of walking and standing. Building in rest stops makes the day more enjoyable for everyone.

## Combining with Tirumala

Many pilgrims do this local tour on the day before or after their Tirumala darshan. Trying to fit Tirumala and all five local temples into one day is possible only rarely and usually ends up feeling rushed. Two separate days is more comfortable.
